# Source tiers, fact-checkers, and poll reading

Reference for `skills/current-events-briefing/SKILL.md`. Organizations change;
verify a source's current status before leaning on it.

## Source tiers

| Tier | Use it for | Examples of the kind | Never use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Primary record | Exact words, official numbers | Transcript or recording, official results, court documents, statistics agencies | Interpretation |
| 2. Independent fact-checkers | Whether a claim is true, per-side tallies | Organizations with a published method and rating scale | A "who won" verdict |
| 3. Wire services and major outlets | What happened, sequence, context | National and international newsrooms with corrections policies | Final verdicts while the event is live |
| 4. Registered polls | Vote intention, approval, viewer reactions | Pollsters that publish method, sample, margin, and registration | Trends across different pollsters |
| 5. Named analysts and columnists | "Who did better" reads | Columnists from across the spectrum, attributed by name | Facts not in tiers 1-4 |
| 6. Partisan and campaign outlets | What a camp claims | Party channels, campaign sites, openly aligned media | Any verdict or fact |

Search tiers 2 and 4 with the organization name plus the event, and read the
page itself; snippets of a live-check page often predate the final tally.

## Choosing fact-checkers

- Prefer signatories of the International Fact-Checking Network (IFCN) Code of
  Principles: nonpartisanship and fairness; transparency of sources, funding,
  and methodology; an open and honest corrections policy.
- Use at least two checkers when they exist, and report each checker's counts
  separately instead of summing scales that differ.
- Illustrative national examples (check current status): Brazil - Agência
  Lupa, Aos Fatos, Estadão Verifica, Projeto Comprova; United States -
  PolitiFact, FactCheck.org, AP Fact Check; multinational - AFP Fact Check,
  Reuters Fact Check.
- A checker's "live" page that says checks are in progress is not a tally.
  Report "roundup not published yet" and when it is likely to appear.

## Reading a poll

Report, in this order:

1. Pollster, fieldwork dates, sample size, method (phone, face to face, online
   panel), and margin of error with its confidence level (usually 95%).
2. Registration where the law requires it. In Brazil, electoral polls are
   registered with the electoral courts before publication and the release
   carries the registration number. The United States has no such legal
   registration; membership in the AAPOR Transparency Initiative is a voluntary
   disclosure signal.
3. Total votes vs valid votes. Valid votes exclude blank and null ballots;
   second-round headlines often use valid votes, so two releases of one poll
   can show different numbers.
4. The lead against the margin. When the two candidates' intervals overlap
   (a lead no larger than about twice the stated margin), say the race is a
   statistical tie ("empate técnico" in Brazilian coverage) and name no leader.
5. Movement only against the same pollster's previous wave, and only when the
   change exceeds about 1.4 times the stated margin (the margin of a difference
   between two independent samples); smaller shifts are "no clear change".
   Different pollsters are separate series.

## Timing cues for follow-ups

- Live debates often end late at night local time; fact-check roundups tend to
  land overnight or the next morning, analyst consensus the next day.
- Polls that can show an event's effect need fieldwork after it, typically
  two to seven days later; a poll released the next morning was usually fielded
  before the event.
- Schedule a "send me when it is out" run after the relevant window, in the
  user's timezone.
